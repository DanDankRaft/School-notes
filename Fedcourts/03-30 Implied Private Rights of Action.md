# Cort v. Ash
422 US 66 (1975)

- 18 USC § 610 makes it a criminal offense for any corporation to make a contribution or expenditure with connection to a presidential election.
- Defendant, chairman of the board of Bethlehem Steel Corp. reprinted a long political spiel and mailed it to every shareholder in Bethlehem alongside their dividend checks. He did it right before the 1972 presidential election.
- Plaintiff was a shareholder in Bethlehem who received the ad and sued, arguing that as a shareholder, he had an *implied private right of action* under the statute.
## Legal Question
- Was there an implied private right of action under this statute?
## Holding
- No.
- Test for implied private right of action (four factors):
	- Does the statute create a federal right in favor of the plaintiff? Is he part of a class receiving *especial benefit* from the statute?
	- Is there any indication of legislative intent to create a private right of action?
	- Is it consistent with the underlying purpose of the statute to create a remedy for the plaintiff?
	- Is this a cause of action in an area traditionally relegated to state law, such that it would be inappropriate to infer a cause of action solely based on federal law?

- Applying here:
	- Criminal statutes are **usually** not interpreted to create a private cause of action - look at it in context.
	- The main purpose of the act was to remove the influence of money from politics.. not to create some sort of private right for shareholders at a corporation.
	- Creating this federal penalty does not really help us to get rid of money from politics. At most, an executive could just "borrow" money from the corporation and pay it back once he's fined.
	- **Corporations are "creatures of state law"** - regulations on corporations are presumed to be the domain of the states.

# Cannon v. University of Chicago
441 US 677 (1979)
- Plaintiff sued a medical school for denying her admission.
- She wanted *injunctive relief* - to be admitted to the school!
- Title IX § 901: "No person in the United States shall, on the basis of sex, be secluded from participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any education program or activity receiving federal financial assistance."
## Legal Question
- Does Title IX create an implied right of action?
## Majority holding
- Yes.
- First factor: statute creates a right to not be discriminated against on the basis of sex!
- Second factor: This came right after Title IV of the Civil Rights Act. Congress saw that the courts recognized a private right of action under that statute, and still took inspiration from it.
	- Takeaway: When considering legislative intent, also take into account how other laws were already interpreted by the courts at this time period.
- Third factor: the purposes are to prevent discrimination, and to do so by cutting off federal funding. But since the threshold for cutting funding is so high, we need a private right of action to achieve the first purpose.
- Fourth factor: **the federal government is the main protector from discrimination.**
## Powell Dissent
- This doctrine goes against separation of powers!
- It allows court to effectively legislate from the bench! That's the power of Congress.
- See Article III - if Congress has complete authority over the jurisdiction of the lower courts, that means they also have authority to tell lower courts what kinds of remedies they're allowed to grant.
- Textbook: implied right of action cases mostly arise in administrative law. When the govt creates rules for private actors but no private mechanism for enforcing them.

# Alexander v. Sandoval
532 US 275 (2001)
- Alabama DOJ decided to only administer their drivers license exam in English.
- Plaintiffs sued due to discrimination on the basis of national origin
- Relevant law: Title VI § 602 "each Federal department and agency ... is authorized and directed to effectuate the provisions of \[§ 601\]"
	- § 601 is the "no person shall be subject to discrimination" clause.
## Holding
- No implied private right!
- Focusing on anything other than Congressional intent is bad... even focusing on "contemporary legal context" is bad.
- The intent here: look at the text! There is no "rights-creating language" in § 602!
- What's rights-creating language?
	- "no person shall ..."
	- Focus on the individual protected rather than the person regulated.
- Also: the express provision of an enforcement method is a strong argument that Congress intended to preclude other enforcement methods.
- Administrative regulations may not create a private right of action by themselves, unless Congress created a private right via a statute!
