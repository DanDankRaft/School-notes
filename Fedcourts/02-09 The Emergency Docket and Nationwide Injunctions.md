# Nationwide Injunctions
- Preliminary injunction that applies to everyone - sometimes even everyone outside the United States.


## Article III and Universal Injunctions
- Common criticism - its an injunction that goes beyond the parties to the case. That violates Article III. It goes beyond the "case and controversy" going before it

- Plaintiff goes to court in immediate aftermath of *Brown v. Board*, arguing that a school district is racially segregating.
	- Can you remedy racial segregation by only applying the remedy to the plaintiff?
	- *Incidental Beneficiaries* - if you relieve someone of segregation in their school district, other black students will be impacted by it too - bc their school will be desegregated!!
	- **If you remedy some constitutional violation - you must effect people beyond the plaintiff to grant the plaintiff complete relief.**
		- Even if every black student in the school district was part in a class action, this affects all white students in the district too!
- Someone sues the state of Texas for racially gerrymandering a single district.
	- Can you grant the plaintiff ***complete relief*** without affecting the rest of the state? No. Bc you'd have to re-draw the rest of the congressional map!
> [!important] When you remedy some constitutional violation - you will inevitably have to effect people beyond the plaintiff to grant the them complete relief.

### Benefits of Nationwide Injunctions
- When the federal government breaks the law, it's the only way to stop unlawful action by the federal executive.
- Complete relief might not be feasible w/o a universal injunction.
- *Quickly* and uniformly resolve an issue affecting the entire country - without piecemeal litigation.
- Puts pressure on the court to resolve hot-button constitutional questions
### Drawbacks of Nationwide Injunctions
- Puts pressure on the court to resolve hot-button constitutional questions.
- Encourages forum shopping - I'll shop for a court more likely to grant the nationwide injunction I'm looking for.
	- *Weakens uniformity* - whether the injunction is held will depend on the circuit!
	- Offensive, non-mutual, collateral estoppel (if a court ruled against the defendant on this controversy before with a different plaintiff, future plaintiffs can use that precedent as binding) is not supposed to apply to the federal government. BUT
- Lack of *percolation* of important constitutional question - gets more people involved in the decision-making process, gets more thinking and debate before getting a decision on the merits.
- Conflict - you can have multiple lower courts making contradictory injunctions on the same issue at the same time.
	- Ex: back when mifepristone was being challenged, the 9th circuit issued an injunction allowing its sale, and the 5th issued an injunction prohibiting it. *On the same day.*
- Nationwide injunctions remove pressure from Congress to act. They're the branch that's supposed to act! It removes accountability on members of Congress...
## Trump v. CASA
606 U.S. __ (2025)
- In January 2025, Trump issued an executive order revoking birthright citizenship for people born on US soil.
- **Trump v. CASA** was actually an emergency docket case! But it had the full process of a typical merits case - with oral arguments and detailed, written opinions from all sides.
### Legal Q
- Does a federal court have the authority to issue a nationwide injunction?
## Holding
- **No**.
- Judiciary Act of 1789 only allowed equitable remedies that already existed in the courts' tradition. Nationwide injunctions did not exist in 1789, so they aren't allowed.
	- They're doing a kind if pseudo-originalism: looking for a historical analogue to find 
	- **Congress *might* be able to authorize the courts to issue nationwide injunctions!**
	- Point of contention between majority and dissent: British "Bill of Peace." Per majority - these only applied to limited, cohesive groups of individuals, so they're more analogous to modern class actions.
- BUT, "broader remedies may provide complete relief".
- Dissent: how in the world are you supposed to guard against unconstitutional executive orders and protect civil rights without being able to issue nationwide injunctions?
	- The courts were so different back then

- Things not decided in this case:
	- Does Article III allow nationwide injunctions?
	- APA vacatur (federal law allowing courts to set aside administrative decisions)
	- What "complete relief" means and if it's allowed
	- What the current state of state standing is; how it relates to third party standing.
	- Whether nationwide class actions are allowed.

- After Trump v. CASA, there were immediately new lawsuits, and the plaintiffs got:
	- nationwide class action certification and injunctive relief
	- a universal injunction for state plaintiffs successfully asserting third party standing.
