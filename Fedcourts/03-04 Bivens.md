# Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents of Federal Bureau of Narcotics
403 US 388 (1971)
- Plaintiff was unlawfully searched and arrested by the Federal Bureau of Narcotics without a warrant.
- Sues for damages - his cause of action is 4th amendment violations. (NOT through §1983 - there is no federal statute being violated here...)
- Lower courts dismissed the complaint.
	- Defense's argument: plaintiff should've sued under state tort law, and used 4th amendment to argue that defendants do not have sovereign immunity? or privilege? not sure.
## Legal Qs
- Does an individual's violation of their 4A rights grant a federal cause of action for damages?
- Does sovereign immunity extend to federal officials who are sued in their official capacity but act unconstitutionally?
## Holding
> [!info] Bivens creates a cause of action against federal officials for violating constitutional rights
- Plaintiff has a valid claim, case remanded on the second question.
- The 4th amendment goes beyond merely banning conduct that would be banned by state law.
	- State laws could actually undermine the 4th amendment interest
- We have a long history of treating damages as the ordinary remedy for a violation of individual liberties.
## Concurrence (J. Harlan)
- It had often been presumed that you can bring a cause of action directly under the constitution if you're asking for future relief in equity.
- Under *Lyons* - he's not eligible at all for future relief. He needs to show that there's a (high probability? not sure) that this injury will happen again.
- *We have to provide Bivens some sort of present relief*
## Dissent (J. Black)
- The only entity that can determine who can sue is Congress, and Congress hasn't said anything at all.
# Davis v. Passman
442 US 228 (1979)
- Title VII exempted discrimination against congressional employees.
- Plaintiff sued a congressman for firing her for being a woman, under 5th Amendment Due Process Clause (actually an equal protection claim, reverse-incorporatied)
- **Holding:** there was a valid Bivens action here.
# Carlson v. Green
446 US 14 (1980)
- Plaintiff sued the director of the Federal Bureau of Prisons for violation 8th amendment via a *Bivens* act.
- Problem: Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA) also existed, enabled plaintiff to sue the US directly here.
- **Holding**: defendant wins!
	- 8th amendment claims are fine under *Bivens*, but...
	- *Bivens* actions are precluded when Congress has provided an alternative remedy as a substitute for recovery directly under the constitution
	- BUT, FTCA did not meet that standard - not explicit enough!

# Comparison: Bivens v. §1983

|                       | Bivens                                                                                                                           | §1983                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Target<br>(Defendant) | Federal officials                                                                                                                | State officials,<br>Local officials,<br>Localities<br>Anyone acting "under color of **state law**"                                                                                               |
| Scope                 | 4th Amendment (*Bivens*),<br>5th Amendment: Equal Protection via reverse incorporation (*Passman*),<br>8th Amendment (*Carlson*) | Any constitutional claim<br>Federal statutory claim<br>International treaties? debatable                                                                                                         |
| Remedy                | **ONLY** damages - compensatory and punitive                                                                                     | Declaratory Relief,<br>Injunctive Relief,<br>Damages - incl. compensatory and punitive damages (The whole retrospective/prospective thing only applies when you're suing via Ex Parte Young)<br> |
> [!info] Federal officials cannot be sued under §1983.
> In the real world, sometimes federal officials are determined to be acting "under color of state law," but it's very rare and will never happen in this class.

> [!info] Bivens only covers the 4th, 5th, and 8th amendments, not any other constitutional claims

## Why did the court create *Bivens*, instead of waiting for a statutory version, a la §1983?
- It's been a century since §1983 passed!
- The court was in a period of wanting to open up the courts to as many people as possible
- Lingering question: would it have been better if Congress took this up instead?

# Preclusion by Alternative Remedy
# Bush v. Lucas
462 US 367
- Federal employee fired for criticizing his own agency, sued under *Bivens*
- The civil service regime already provided a remedy system for this.

- **Holding**: no Bivens action.
	- Alternate remedy test - is there an elaborate remedial system already set up, with careful attention to conflicting policy considerations? This is a question for Congress!
> [!info] A sufficient alternative remedy doesn't need to be as good as Bivens, nor explicitly intended by Congress to act as an alternative to Bivens
- NOTE: Neither Congress nor the Courts never said that those remedies were:
	- Intended to replace *Bivens*
	- As good as *Bivens*
# Shweiker v. Chilicky
487 US 412 (1988)
> [!info] A system that provides only a partial compensatory remedy can be a sufficient alternative remedy
- Plaintiffs' social security benefits had been wrongfully terminated. Benefits were then restored,
- But plaintiff sued for damages for the suffering they endured while benefits were suspended.
- In the meantime, Congress enacted reforms to make sure that benefits would still be awarded while administrative appeals were ongoing
- **Holding:** indistinguishable from *Bush v. Lucas*. Congress set up an alternative remedy.
# Hui v. Castaneda
559 US 799 (2010)
> [!info] A statute that explicitly says it provides an "exlcusive" remedy is a sufficient alternative
- Public Health officer failed to diagnose skin cancer on immigration detainee.
- Federal Tort Claims Act 42 USC § 233(a) - this act provides a remedy "exclusive of any other civil action or proceeding"
- Subtext: the plaintiff wanted *punitive damages* and a *jury*. The Federal Tort Claims Act did not provide that!
- **Holding:** No *Bivens*. FTCA provides an alternative remedy!
	- Subtext: most *Bivens* opinions are contentious, but this opinion was unanimous. Statutory grounds are very strong for precluding *Bivens*

# Sensitive Contexts
- Another way to preclude a *Bivens* claim
## Chappel v. Wallace
462 US 296 (1983)
> [!info] The military is a sensitive context that court's don't delve into, so can't have *Bivens* claims!
> There was at least an internal military structure to deal with discrimination here
- Enlisted men sued their officers for racial discrimination
- **Holding:** the military is a sensitive context where *Bivens* actions are inappropriate.
	- Specifically, can't have people bringing suit against their superior officers.
# United States v. Stanley
483 US 669 (1987)
> [!info] 
- Army Sgt. volunteered for a study, where he was secretly administered LSD, and plaintiff suffered greatly for the rest of his life. Sued under *Bivens*.
- **Holding:** No *Bivens* when injuries "arise out of or are in the course of activity incident to service."
- **Dissent:** This is not a plaintiff-suing-commander case!
