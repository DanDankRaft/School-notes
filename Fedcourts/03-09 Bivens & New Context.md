# Ziglar v. Abbasi
582 US ___ (2017)
- Plaintiffs were unlawfully detained ("hold until cleared" policy) in the aftermath of 9/11.
- Sued two categories - executive officials )AG, FBI Director, ICE Commissioner), and their prison wardens.
- Claims under 5th amendment Due Process
## Legal Question
- Was this a "new context" not covered by prior *Bivens* claims?

## Holding
- Yes, and therefore there were no *Bivens* grounds here.
	- National security context - post 9/11!
	- There was public awareness about this issue and yet Congress stayed silent on a remedy
	- habeas corpus provided a *superior alternative*
- What makes something a new context:
	- implicates a different constitutional right
	- potential "special factors" (see above)
	- judicial precedent provides a less meaningful guide for official misconduct
## Dissent
- *Bivens* is especially important in national security contexts, bc the nature of damages litigation means it will be heard in court after the emergency/emotional circumstances subsided...

# Westfall Act
- Federal Tort Claims Act is the "exclusive remedy" for tortious injuries caused by federal officials, acting within the scope of their office or employment.
- BUT, exception left out for a violation of the Constitution of the United States.
	- The point of this exception - to use *Bivens* framework in such cases!
	- One argument - this exception is also meant to enable more state tort claims in such cases!
	- Weird tension - if there are more readily available state tort claims, then *Bivens* is harder to get. And vice versa...
# Hernandez v. Mesa
589 US ___ (2020)
- A group of Mexican teenagers accidentally crossed the border, plaintiff's son was shot and killed by a border patrol officer.
- His parents sued the officer under *Bivens*, for 4th and 5th amendment violations.
## Legal Question
- This this a "new context"?
### Holding
- Yes!
- Contributing factors:
	- The additional risk of the judiciary intruding into the functioning of the other branches
	- Foreign relations context
	- national security context
	- Congress has repeatedly declined to authorize the award of damages for injuries inflicted outside the border
		- Wait.... but this whole case happened inside the border????
> [!info] Strong signals from the court that they want to overturn *Bivens*
> They don't want to award damages in a context where Congress hasn't created a remedy!

# Egbert v. Boule
596 US ___ (2022)
- Plaintiff was harassed by a border patrol agent. In response to filing a complaint, defendant launched a retaliatory IRS audit of Plaintiff's taxes???
- Plaintiff sued under *Bivens*, 4th and 1st amendment claims
### Legal Questions
- Is there a "new context" here?
- Can you sue for *Bivens* claim under 1A?
## Holding
- Plaintiff loses
> [!info] Bivens is about whether there is any reason to think that Congress might be better equipped to create a damages remedy
- 4A is a new context here:
	- "border security" is a context where Congress is better equipped to create remedies.
	- Govt. has already provided a remedy! (filing a grievance?)
- *Bivens* cannot be used for 1st amendment complaints.


# Factors contributing to "new context" or avoidance of Bivens
- *Literally any argument* for why this is different from the cases where the *Bivens* plaintiff wins.
- National security context
- Foreign relations context
- Border security in particular
- Did Congress provide an alternative remedy
	- If not, is it bc Congress probably doesn't wanna provide one?
	- If not, is Congress better equipped to create one?
