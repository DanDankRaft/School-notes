# Qualified Immunity
- Courts tried using subjective intent as the standard for determining qualified immunity, but it didn't work out!
- Going forward, this is an *objective test* (*Harlow*, *Brosseau*)
	1. Was there a violation of a federal right under these facts?
	2. Knew/reasonably should've known about this violation? Whether it is "clearly established" at the time of the defendant's conduct?
		- Known that the conduct was a violation!

# *Brosseau*
- Officer shot a suspect in the back as they're running away.
### Holding
- Level of generality: low
	- The cases have to be factually similar
		- "whether to shoot a pursued suspect at flight while doing so puts others in danger"


# *Hope v. Pelzer*
- Is it "cruel and unusual" to tie a prisoner to a hitching post for 2-7 hours?
- There was no case with these exact facts.
### Holding
- The facts were so obviously unconstitutional that there didn't need to be any case directly on point.


# *Taylor v. Riojas*
- Inmate was put in a cell with terribly unsanitary conditions - raw sewege, feces, etc.
### Holding
- When the facts are these extreme, that is enough.
