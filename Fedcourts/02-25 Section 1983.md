# 42 U.S.C. § 1983
> Every person who, **under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage**, of any State or Territory, subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States or other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable tot he party injured in an action at law, suit in equity, or other proper proceeding for redress.

History:
- Passed as part of the Ku Klux Act of 1871, under authority of Section 5 of 14th amendment.
- Primarily meant to address constitutional violations by Ku Klux Klan that went unaddressed by local and state authorities during reconstruction.
# Monroe v. Pape
365 US 167 (1961)
## Background
- Plaintiff was heavily mistreated by Chicago police, in violation of due process rights under 14th amendment.
- Brought suit against them using §1983
- Lower courts dismissed complaint, bc the police officers were clearly not acting in accordance with Illinois law.
## Legal Question
- Can §1983 be used when the defendant is violating constitutional rights, but not acting in accordance with any law?
- About what it means for a state official to act "under color of law"
## Holding
- Yes!
- Three purposes of 1983:
	- Override certain state laws
	- Provide a remedy where state law is inadequate
	- **Provide a federal remedy where state remedy was adequate in theory but unavailable in practice.**
- *United States v. Classic*, *Screws v. United States* both set the precedent that acting "under the color of law" includes blatantly misusing power or violating state law, but doing so while "clothed with the authority of state law."

# Gonzaga University v. Doe
536 US 273 (2002)
- Defendants violated FERPA by leaking details about plaintiff's sexual assault.
- Plaintiff sued for damages under § 1983 and won in lower courts
## Holding
- No §1983.
- The section only protects individual rights intentionally created by Congress, not some vague benefits or interests.
- **Plaintiffs bear burden of showing that Congress intended to create a private right via the statute in question.**
- Here: FERPA was enacted under the Spending Clause. The enforcement mechanism is to stop giving funds to the state - not a private cause of action.
# City of Palos Verdes v. Abrams
544 US 113 (2005)
- Showing that a statute creates an individually enforceable right only creates a rebuttable presumption that there's a § 1983 right.
- The important element here is **Congressional intent**. If you can demonstrate lack of Congressional intent, you can rebut the presumption.
