- Procedural Default - when the state court denies the habeas petition for procedural reasons, rather than on the merits. Usually the result of attorney error rather than strategic choice.
# Fay v. Noia
372 US 391 (1963)
- Overturned *Daniels v. Allen*
- A state habeas petition that was denied on procedural grounds does not foreclose a federal review!
- Only thing that forecloses federal review: if the petitioner intentionally waived a constitutional claim that they're now bringing in a federal habeas petition.
# Wainwright v. Sykes
433 US 72 (1977)
- Petitioner was informed of his Miranda rights and gave testimony while drunk.
- Even though there was evidence that he was drunk, his attorney failed to question the admissibility of his testimony.

- Florida had a "contemporaneous objection rule" - you must object to admission of evidence before trial.
- Federal law - requires a hearing in a state criminal trial before the admission of this type of evidence
## Legal Q
- Did petitioner "waive his constitutional right" and is therefore ineligible for a federal habeas petition?
## Holding
- Yes.
- Sets up a standard for an *Erie*-style review when a state procedural law conflicts with a federal procedural law:
	- consider "comity and concerns for the orderly administration of criminal justice"
- Reasons to adopt Florida law (contemporaneous objection) over the federal law:
	- contemporaneous objection is common!
	- Requires challenges to be litigated when recollections of the witnesses are freshest
	- Contributes to finality - not turning state trial on the merits to the first stop out of many!
	- Prevents "sandbagging" from D attorneys - they don't raise any objections that don't help them to get a "not guilty" verdict until after the initial trial, and then use these as constitutional objections in habeas petitions... with the potential to get an injunction for immediate release.

# House v. Bell
547 US 518 (2006)
- If a constitutional violation resulted in the conviction of someone who is actually innocent, then there are grounds to grant a habeas petition on a showing of actual innocence, even if the state habeas petition was refused bc of a procedural default.
