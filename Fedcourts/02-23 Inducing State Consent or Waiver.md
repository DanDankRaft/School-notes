# Alden v. Maine
527 US 706 (1999)
- A group of Maine probation officers sued the state under Fair Labor Standards Act.
- Maine argued sovereign immunity in its own state courts.
- This was the first major state sovereignty case after *Seminole Tribe*.
### Legal Question
- Does a state maintain sovereign immunity, even in its own state courts?
### Holding
- The state has sovereign immunity!
- Explaining earlier decisions where it seems like we ignored state sovereign immunity:
	- **Sovereign immunity is a waiveable defense. By not raising it, the state is effectively waiving it!!!**
- Viewing this as waiveable makes sense if you think about why we have state sovereign immunity:
	- To protect states rights from Congress
	- To ensure they won't go bankrupt
- We presume that states will obey the Constitution and federal laws in good faith. See: Constitution, Article VI, Clause 2.
## Dissent
- We have to adopt a living constitutionalism approach here..
- The state is not sovereign with respect to "the national objective of the FLSA"

# College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board
527 US 666 (1999)
- This is a continuation of a case we discussed last class??
## Background
- Trademark Remedy Clarification Act (TRCA) - federal law waiving 11th amendment immunity for misleading advertising from state agencies.
## Legal Q
- Do states "constructively waive" sovereign immunity by engaging in commercial activity?
## Holding
- No.
- Explicitly overturning an older case (*Parden*, 377 US 184 (1964))
## Dissent
- The power to regulate the commercial activity of state actors is rooted in the necessary and proper clause. Otherwise, how can they regulate interstate commerce?

# Sossamon v. Texas
563 US 277 (2011)
- Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act of 2000 - prohibits a substantial burden on the religious exercise of an institutionalized person, absent a compelling state interest, if "the substantial burden is imposed in a program or activity that receives Federal financial assistance." The state also allowed private cause of action, with monetary damages.
- Sossamon was a Texas prisoner who sued under the act
## Legal Q
- Is "appropriate relief against a government" sufficiently unambiguous to require state waiver of sovereign immunity?
## Holding
- No.
- When the state is sovereign, merely imposing monetary damages is not enough!
