## Plaintiff B's Official Capacity Claims
- We have to confront state sovereign immunity!
- Our only venue here is abrogation under 14th amendment.

- Test for abrogation:
	- Clear statement
	- Valid under §5 of 14th amendment.
		- Find what violation Congress is targeting - "congruence and proportionality" (*City of Boerne*)

- Arguments for clear statement:
	- It's clear. The statute explicitly say that employer = the state. (*Seminole Tribe*)
- Arguments against clear statement:
	- It doesn't say anything about abrogation or sovereign immunity..

- Plaintiff: what right is this protecting?
	- First amendment - strict scrutiny! (*Connick*)
	- If the standard of scrutiny is heightened, then Congress can rely on a wide variety of state, private, and local violations (*Hibbs*, *Tennessee v. Lane*)
		- There is tons of Congressional evidence here - seven months of hearings, hundreds of violations over a five-year period from state, local, and private employers in 27 states!
	- Therefore, abrogation is OK
- Defendant: what right is this protecting?
	- The violation being targeted is too broad - SCOTUS said the only protected employee speech is "public concern," but this covers all employee speech - which only receives rational basis!
	- If the standard of scrutiny is rational basis, then Congress must demonstrate a pattern of irrational conduct, and only by the state! (*Kimel, Garrett, Coleman, Florida Prepaid*)
	- The evidence here - at least two dozen incidents over five years.
	- Therefore, abrogation is not OK.
	- Responses:
		- First, the employee speech here was about public concern, so even if the statute goes too far in some cases, our speech is still protected. (*Tennessee v. Lane*)
		- Second, two dozen incidents over five years is still demonstrating a widespread pattern of constitutional violations!!
