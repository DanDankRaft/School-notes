# Herrera v. Collins
- Petitioner was accused of capital murder, and sentenced to death.
- Filed a Habeas petition claiming that he was actually innocent, and that his dead brother actually did it.
- It's been 10 years since the conviction. This is second Habeas petition.
	- How could he file a second Habeas petition? This is before AEDPA!
- *Brady* violation - the prosecution has evidence that is related to the case and could be exculpatory, they have to hand it to the defendant.
	- The lower courts reject the *Brady* claim.
	- SCOTUS doesn't dig into it..
- The claim here - **a freestanding claim of actual innocence**

**Freestanding claim of actual innocence** - claim of factual innocence, not tied to any constitutional violation in earlier proceedings.
- This is different from *legal* innocence - where it's impossible to determine based on the facts that the defendant was innocent beyond reasonable doubt, but we can't convict them because of some legal technicality - usually a constitutional violation.
- Here, we merely argue that the *jury just got it wrong*.
Why should we allow a freestanding claim of actual innocence?
- 8th amendment - cruel and unusual punishment
- 14th amendment - "nor shall any State deprive any person of life ... without due process of law."
	- Worries about juries being biased or getting things wrong,
	- We want an opportunity to present new evidence
	- Criminal law equivalent of allowing JNOV
	- No reasonable alternative - executive clemency is not really a feasible option in most cases!
Why should we *not* allow a freestanding claim of actual innocence?
- Habeas is about *legal* errors, not making sure that the jury got the facts correctly.
- Even in other practice, we don't question the trial court's factual determinations.
- We want to prevent a flood of cases that could overwhelm the courts, rely on stale evidence..
- Alternatives exist! Executive clemency, state courts, legislative action, the media, parole board
	- They've already had lots of review!
Why are we skeptical of the alternatives?
- Pardons are politically unpopular! State judges run on being "tough on crime!"
	- Especially here, where the petitioner was convicted of killing police officers.
	- **The only times when a death penalty is even going to be an option is in a homicide case, where every party with a chance to grant you an alternative remedy is **
- Some states don't even have a procedure for hearing an innocence claim! Not even in a parole board!

How is this different from a sufficiency of the evidence claim? (*Jackson v. VA*)
- Only about the evidence on the record at the time of the original trial!
- Requires an independent constitutional violation: proof beyond a reasonable doubt!

## Is there a freestanding claim of actual innocence?
- Maybe? Probably not.
- If there is, this guy wouldn't get it..

# Problem 34
- Looking at two law proposals:
	- federal courts shall entertain petition for habeas if the person is (1) in custody in violation of the Constitution or laws or treaties of the United States or (2) raises a freestanding claim of actual innocence.
	- Federal courts shall only entertain petition for habeas if the person is *both* in custody in violation of the Constitution or laws or treaties AND raises a freestanding claim of actual innocence.
		- Fun fact: this is the current standard of the 5th circuit. Held that way by a panel!
