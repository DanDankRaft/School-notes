# Amendment XI
> The Judicial power of the United States shall not be construed to extend to any suit in law or equity, commenced or prosecuted against one of the United States by Citizens of another State, or by Citizens or Subjects of any Foreign State.

# Coleman v. Court of Appeals of Maryland
2 US 419 (1793)
- A SC merchant sued the state of GA to recover supplies from some contract.
- SCOTUS found that they had jurisdiction to adjudicate this, despite GA's sovereign immunity. 
- This generated so much backlash that it led to the passage of the 11th amendment.

# Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida
517 US 44 (1996)
## Background
- Indian Gaming Regulatory Act - imposes a duty to negotiate in good faith on states to allow indian reservations to establish gambling institutions, including a clause making it judicially enforceable by allowing Indian tribes to sue in federal court for a state failing to negotiate in good faith.
	- Based on the Indian Commerce Clause of the US constitution.
## Legal Question
- Is the enforcement clause constituional? Or is it a violation of state sovereign immunity (Amendment XI)
## Holding
- No. The clause violates state sovereign immunity.
- Reading Amendment XI broadly - each state is sovereign, and sovereignty means individuals cannot sue them without their consent.
- Test for whether Congress violated Amendment XI:
	- Did it unequivocally express its intent to abrogate the immunity
	- Has it acted pursuant to a valid exercise of power?

- Application here:
	1. Yes, it did express intent to abrogate immunity.
	2. The only valid way to abrogate immunity is on 14th amendment grounds (see combination of clause 1 and clause 5 of that amendment)
- In reaching this decision, they explicitly overturn *Union Gas*, a semi-recent plurality decision that allowed the Commerce Clause to abrogate 11th amendment.
