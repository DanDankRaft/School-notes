# Williams v. Taylor
529 U.S. 362 (2000)

## Legal Q
- What standard of review does the AEDPA require courts to use?
## Holding (majority)
- The state court decision must be "substantially different from the relevant precedent of this Court"
- Not enough to be incorrect, must be a plainly unreasonable reading of SCOTUS precedent.
- Only count holdings, not dicta!

## Dissent
- All the AEDPA does is set a "mood" of deference to state courts. If a decision is still wrong, we still get to review it!
# White v. Woodall
572 U.S. 415 (2014)

- Woodall sentenced to death under kidnapping, raping, and murdering a 16yo.
- At the penalty phase, he did not tesify. His attorney asked the judge to not hold his lack of testimony against him, as (might be) required under the 5th amendment. Judge rejected this request, state supreme court affirmed.
- This case is a petition of habeas corpus over that decision.

- Caselaw establishes that no-adverse-inference instruction is required at the guilt phase, that no evidence from an involuntary psychiatric examination can be introduced at the penalty phase, and that a defendant's silence at sentencing cannot be considered in some factual circumstances.

## Legal Question
- Are the courts allowed to grant a petition of habeas corpus here under the AEDPA?
## Holding
- No.
- This case is not "contrary to" the actual holdings of any of these cases.
- This reading is not "beyond any possibility for fairminded disagreement"
- AEDPA only provides remedy when courts unreasonably apply a precedent. It does not provide a remedy when courts refuse to extend a precedent.
