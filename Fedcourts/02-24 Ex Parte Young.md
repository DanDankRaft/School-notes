# Ex Parte Young
209 US 123 (1908)

- Minnesota enacted a law setting the price of train tickets.
- Plaintiffs sued the state and Young, MN attorney general, arguing that the laws are unconstitutional. They won in federal court.
- Young tried enforcing the law via state court anyways. He was found guilty of acting in contempt of court and put in prison. Petitioned SCOTUS for writ of habeas corpus.

## Legal Question
- How the hell do you sue a state for doing something unconstitutional?
## Holding
- You can sue a state official, carrying out the unconstitutional task on behalf of the state.
	- **This way, you can get an injunctive remedy against the individual official.**
- The Supremacy Clause says that the constitution overrides all unconstitutional state laws.
- Therefore, a state official carrying out the duties of an unconstitutional law is not acting as an agent of the state - because the state cannot enforce a law that is unconstitutional!
- Also, it makes sense to have a preliminary injunction here bc this law has a criminal penalty... do we really want to make one of the company's employees go to prison in order to litigate this?
## Dissent
- This is the end of federalism! Loely district courts can now just render state laws unconstitutional!!!

# Whole Woman's Health v. Jackson (2021)
- Texas SB 8 allows any person to sue anyone else for aiding or abetting abortion. Bounty enforcement.
	- No state official was actually enforcing the law!
- Legal q: in order to render the law unconstitutional, who do you sue?
- Holding: you must sue state officials who are enforcing the law. Which officials those are is up to state law and state courts.
# Edelman v. Jordan
415 US 651 (1974)
- Plaintiff sued state officials in Illinois for violating some federal disability benefits law (Aid to the Aged, Blind, or Disabled Act (AABD))
- Won lawsuit, but demanded monetary relief for retroactive benefits they did not receive.
- Legal question: can they do that? Under *Ex Parte Young*
## Holding
- No.
- It's fine to demand an injunctive relief that will lose the state a lot of money but that's different :/
- Useful questions:
	- Is this money spent just to comply with the law, or is it compensation for plaintiffs?
	- Will this be paid by the individual defendant or by the state?
# Milliken v. Bradley (II)
433 US 267 (1977)
- *Ex Parte Young* case about school desegregation. Lower court ordered costly state programs such as remedial reading programs.
- Legal Q: is this barred by Edelman?
- Holding: no. 
	- Just bc it's "compensatory" doesn't mean it's prohibited.
	- **This is a plan that operates *prospectively* rather than *retrospectively***
