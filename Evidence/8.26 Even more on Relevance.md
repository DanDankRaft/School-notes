# Review from Yesterday
- Rule 401 Test for Relevance:
	- (a) probability - does this evidence make some fact any more or less likely. Does it move the needle *at all*?
	- (b) materiality - does this fact matter to determining the outcome of this case?
- Rule 403 - Countervailing considerations
	- Even if the evidence is relevant, there are countervailing considerations that outweigh letting in this evidence.
	- Today, we're focusing on one consideration: *unfair prejudice*


# Old Chief v. United States
SCOTUS 1997
- Very useful case for seeing how SCOTUS understands relevance and the FRE
## Background
- 18 USC 992(g)(1) - prohibits possession of firearm by anyone with prior felony conviction.
	- In a prior case, he was convicted of felony assault
- Common practice - D, fearing prejudice from jury, will enter stipulation agreement with prosecution where:
	- D **stipulate** prior felony conviction
	- P doesn't enter any evidence regarding the nature of the felony.
- Here, D made this stipulation but P still entered evidence saying that D was convicted of assault.
	- P's strategy: if jury sees evidence of assault, they'll think he's a violent person and think he's more likely to commit assault.
	- Why that's a problem: FRE 404 - Character Evidence Rule. TL;DR: Evidence of a person's character is not admissible simply to argue that he acted in accordance with that character on this occasion.
## Legal Q
- Does this evidence become irrelevant under FRE 401?
- Can such evidence be excluded for unfair prejudice under FRE 403?
## Holding
- Item excluded.
- 401 Objection: DENIED
	- **Just because a fact is not in dispute does not mean that a party is not entitled to bring evidence**
- 403 Objection: GRANTED
	- The probative value of a single item of evidence is not weighed in isolation, but by comparing alternatives that can prove the same fact.
		- Here, D's admission would've been both more probative and less prejudicial
	- While letting each side choose how they want to prove their point is important
		- E.g., by making a rich, colorful story 
		- E.g., by engaging the jury emotionally - so that they're motivated to actually enforce the law
		- E.g., meeting jury expectation of getting forensic evidence (*CSI Effect*)
	- ...this evidence doesn't contribute to any of this. All the prosecution wanted to admit was evidence that he had a prior conviction for assault. This doesn't do all of that.
	- How do we deal with all of this under Rule 403?
		- "The risk of unfair prejudice did substantially outweigh the *discounted* probative value of the record of conviction"
			- Evidence is not an island...
		- In isolation, the probative value of evidence that D was convicted of assault is extremely high to proving that he had a felony conviction. And has some unfair prejudice
			- Probative value = 10
			- Unfair prejudice = 5
		- The stipulation:
			- Probative value = 10
			- Unfair prejudice = 2
		- Court says: *because there is an alternative form of evidence for this with the same probative value*, we discount it
			- Assault conviction:
				- Discounted probative value = 2
	- Real world application: "if you can prove the same thing with a less prejudicial item of evidence, use the less prejudicial evidence!" BUT only if it's not important to tell the story of the case.

# Fields v. City of Chicago
N.D.Ill 2018
### Background
- P was shot in his own home by cops, who then delayed calling him an ambulance and searched his home without a warrant.
- D's account: they feared for their life bc P charged at them with a "silver object." They thought it would be a gun, but it turned out to just be a wrench.
- P seeks damages under §1983
## Motions In Liminie (MILs)
1.  Evidence that the cops would suffer negative professional consequences if they lose the trial.
	- Denied in part. This is irrelevant, and would confuse the issues. But they can testify to personal liability
2. Evidence relating to the problem of gun violence in Chicago generally
	- Denied. Qualified Immunity's objectively reasonable standard concerns the specific circumstances of this shooting
3. Evidence that the neighborhood is "high crime"
	- Admitted. Relevant to the standard "in light of the facts and circumstances in which they were confronted"
4. Evidence that P used to be in a gang
	- Denied. Probative value far outweighed by unfair prejudice.
5. Evidence regarding a code of silence among Chicago cops about covering up misconduct
	- Denied. Little probative value
6. Evidence regarding insufficiency of the investigation
	- Denied. Outweighed by unfair prejudice and confusion
7. Evidence that any non-D engaged in misconduct
	- Denied. Irrelevant
8. Evidence that D should have used any other method of force.
	- Denied. Little probative and unduly prejudicial bc reasonableness of officer's actions are evaluated from their perspective.
9. Evidence regarding how Chicago PD trains and disciplines officers
	- Denied. Irrelevant and prejudicial
10. Evidence that D's misconduct was racially motivated
	- Denied without prejudice. Racial prejudice is not relevant for determining whether the officer acted reasonably, but may be relevant if they are found liable and P then asks for punitive damages.

# Ballou v. Henri Studios, Inc.
5th Cir. 1981
- In car crash case:
	- Nurse testified that minutes before crash, D did not have alcohol in his system.
	- Alcohol test showed BAC of 0.24%
- Legal Q: should the judge exclude the BAC evidence, as they carry little probative value in light of the nurse's testimony?
- Holding: NO. Rule 403 does not ask judges whether evidence is believable. It asks them merely to weigh probative value, assuming that the evidence is true.
