# Dworkin and Disability
- Disability is a real challenge for Dworkin; it's a mirror image of expensive taste. It's a case where we feel as if "expensive taste" feels intuitively justified.
	- Remember, Dworkin's issue with expensive taste is that it requires taking more resources, which compromises others' ability to attain welfare. His response is that those people should "grow up," get the same resources as everyone else 
	- But... you could say the same thing about people with disabilities.
- **Conversion deficit** - people with expensive taste or disabilities need more resources to attain the same level of welfare than others.

## How does Dworkin deal with this? 
- Every other type of equality already struggles with disabilities
	- Ex: "Tiny Tim." An incredibly disabled guy who's always supper happy. Under equality of welfare, Tiny Tim deserves *less* than the average person!
	- Ex: a person who is severely disabled, but equalizing their welfare requires so many resources that it makes the rest of society significantly worse off.
		- Especially if you have an issue with *marginal utility*. At the beginning, each dollar improves their life significantly, but the marginal utility of each dollar decreases, the more dollars you drop on them. So you might get to a point where 90% of the resources you spend only improve their utility by a small amount.
	- Ex: a severely disabled person receives a lot of resources to alleviate their disability, but they would prefer to use the money to fulfill some expensive taste instead.
- Resources also includes your "internal resources" - your mind and body. Disabled people don't deserve "more resources," they just need more external resources in order to have the same amount of total resources as everyone else.
	- Bracha - that's cheating! At this point, you're no longer equalizing resources; you're equalizing something else!
	- **That something else is Capabilities**
- Looking ahead - what counts as "adequate compensation" in torts depends on the conception of equality we're trying to achieve!

# More Critiques of Spaces of Equality (Sen article, before delving into Capabilities)
## Utilitarianism (Utilitarian Equality)
- Why does he even call it a theory of equality?
	- We still treat everyone equally!
	- Economists say it's an equality of marginal utility:
		- If you try to maximize overall utility, you'll eventually reach a point where each individual's marginal utility is the same.
		- Basically, the algorithm to maximize utility goes as follows:
			- Divide our total resources into some small unit.
			- For each unit, find the person who would gain the most utility from receiving that resource.
			- Repeat until we run out of resources OR until everyone's marginal utility is the same.
			- At the end *marginal utility* across society will be much more equal than before.
## Equality of Welfare (Total Utility Equality)

### Urgency of Preferences
- There are some different human interests where it's just wrong to weigh them on the same scale.

# Equal Capabilities
- We need to focus on also equalizing something inbetween resources and welfare.
## What are Capabilities?
- Nussbaum - capabilities include both "internal capabilities" - your talents, skills, and "external capabilities" - how much your social/political/economnic environment enables you to exercise your internal capabilities. She tries to attach capabilities to some concrete real-world value but kind of fails.
- Cohen - capabilities are *midfare*
	- Midfare is your internal resources as a person. Something that comes after external means but before the final good results.
	- You can think of a pipeline. Resource -> Midfare -> Welfare.
		- Ex: food is the resource. The feeling of contentness is the welfare. The nutritional value is the midfare.

- If capabilities are *skills*, then what skills count as capabilities that we should try to equalize? We have to create a list.
	- The problem: if we create a list, we (indirectly) outline what counts as the good life. We're losing the subjectivity that Equality of Welfare addresses.
	- **This is why many liberals don't like equal capabilities - it violates equal respect.** Classical liberalism is all about being "equal about the right, not about the good."
	- You end up with a regime that tells people how to live their life.

- Nussbaum - it's about *dignity*. For humans, that means human dignity. We should equalize the skills that enable humans to live a good life.
	- It even extends to other species. Cats should get a dignified cat life.

- Equal Capabilities believers really strongly believe in non-commensurability. There are different spaces of different human interests and capacities and we cannot trade between those.
	- Ex: you can't trade off someone'bodily health for play.

## The Function of Distribution
- Function of distribution: Equality of Capabilities believers don't really believe in equalizing capabilities across everyone. They care about making sure everyone crosses a minimum threshhold.
	- aka a **sufficiency** or **threshhold** function.
- What's the problem with sufficiency?
	- Who gets to pick what the threshhold is?
	- If the threshhold is high, it becomes very difficult to get everyone to reach that threshhold.
		- Especially in a world with limited resources - should we spend immense resources on a few people to get them to almost hit some threshhold?
	- If the threshhold is low, you can meet it while having immense inequality between individuals.
- Why should we not care about what happens beyond that threshhold?


> [!info] For any conception of equality to not devolve into authoritarianism, we must leave space for *free choice*.
> Different theorists disagree on what choices people should be allowed to make.

- In Equal Opportunity, we just throw people to the wolves and let the free market play things out.
- In Equal Capability, we first equalize people's capabilities, give them some tools that allows them to make more choices, and then let them make those choices.
	- But, of course, by choosing which capabilities we want to grant people

- Cohen: if we think of capabilities too much in terms of freedom, we develop an "over-athletic" conception of capabilities. Where capabilities are about being able to do stuff. This conflates two things:
	- Capabilities proper - freedom to do and achieve things in the world. Freedom to do things.
	- State of being of humans in the world - freedom from disease, freedom from hunger. **These freedoms are more fundamental (ie, more important) than typical capabilities.** It tends to be the more privileged who care about positive freedoms than negative freedoms.
- This is a response to Nussbaum.
- Nussbaum says: those are not separate things. If you have some negative freedom, you gain the capability to *enjoy it*. IE, if you're free from hunger, you're free to enjoy that lack of hunger.
