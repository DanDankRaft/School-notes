# Educational Accommodation and Disruptive Equity
- Education is not about competition, it's about individual growth
- He doesn't merely view education as a tool to get other stuff.
- Two opposing conceptions of education:
	- Meritocratic view - providing resources to prove their own merit, and allow people to reach their "full potential."
		- K-12 education is a *race*
		- This is a purely procedural view. It's not about some distributive notion of justice, but a discussion about how to obtain fairness. This is where the courts fail to come up with a workable standard for implementing IDEA.
	- Democratic model - education is a good in itself. Ensure everyone is equipped "as people." Education is a goal in its own merit.
		- Squarely within the capabilities camp. Education is a capability!
		- The good we're distributing is access to educational development.


- Consistency: if we don't have one consistent system, we run into problems that don't necessarily apply to the proportional progress principle in a vacuum.
	- Ex: what if some school districts keep the current meritocratic distribution while others adopt proportional progress? Brighter students in the former category may struggle to get into elite universities.

- There's a social utility to having the brightest engineers design the most important machines, the brightest doctors doing the most important surgeries, etc. Without this meritocratic system we lose that sorting mechanism.

# Substantive Distributive Justice
- Syed's position is a mathematical ratio that takes into account both maximization and maximin.


- IDEA and ADA single out a single dimension of disadvantage - disability. It tells us to exclusively focus on that.
- Is it worthwhile to keep focusing on this dimension once a student crosses past being average? Should we focus on other dimensions of disadvantage?
	- One answer: No. The moment you treat the average level, we shouldn't treat you as disabled for distributive purposes anymore.
