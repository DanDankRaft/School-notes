# Some preliminary thoughts

# Theme One - Unequitable Distribution of FOSS
- If you look at the kinds of FOSS projects that are widely successful, they tend to be oriented towards developers, and not end-users.
	- Usually, that end-user software exists, it just kind of sucks!
- The main result of this: a production problem - inequality 
	- There's an inequality of capabilities. Think of software that people need to do their jobs: Photoshop, After Effects, Westlaw. The presence of software is what you need to do your job. The cost of software is the cost of being able to engage in your job!
	- **End-user software is a natural monopoly**
	- Often, people are trained in the *industry standard.* You're not a photo editor, you're really good at photoshop. You're not a legal researcher - you're an expert at westlaw/lexis. And having to switch to a different option - even if a viable one exists - is incredibly difficult or impractical.
	- With software developers, this is not the case!
		- One - because the skills you learn as a software developer make you uniquely positioned to learn new software and switch to alternatives.
		- Two - because the industry standards in software development are already FOSS!
	- Why do FOSS developers not create alternatives to what's out there?

- Priority distribution: focus on FOSS development of software in the fields where its absence is the most harmful.
	- 3D modeling. There is no need to create a FOSS competitor to Blender, bc Blender already exists!
	- Game development - increased attention on Godot once Unity's monopoly became particularly egregious. 
	- Downsides of existing prioritization:
		- Once a project becomes successful, it tends to attract more developers - not less. So does it actually work?
		- Once a free but not FOSS alternative exists, it tends to kill interest!

- Policy to encourage prioritization? Not sure what that would look like.
- Equality of Capabilities approach - turn everyone into a developer! Let people create software to suit their own needs
	- Main objection: it violates equal respect. Even in a post-AI world, becoming a developer is a ton of work, that most people would find deeply unenjoyable. I don't want to inflict that on people!
	- But part of me is quite sympathetic to that approach tbh
