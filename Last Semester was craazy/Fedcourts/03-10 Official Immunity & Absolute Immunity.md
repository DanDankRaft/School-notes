# Official Immunity
- Applies when: sued in *personal capacity* for damages.
	- Sovereign immunity doesn't apply when someone gets sued in their personal capacity!
- Why do we have official immunity? (*Scheuer*)
	- It is unjust to subject an officer to liability for actions which require discretion
	- The threat of liability would deter officers from exercising decisiveness and good judgement.

# Scheuer v. Rhodes
416 US 232 (1974)
- Plaintiffs were estates of three students who died in the Kent State Massacre.
- §1983 action!
- They sued two groups of people *in their personal capacity*:
	- Why personal capacity? If you sue an official in their official capacity, that can be barred by the 11th amendment.
	- Higher level officials like the governor, state AG, etc.
	- Members of the Ohio National Guard involved in the shootings.
- Lower court dismissed due to official immunity
## Legal Q
- What type of immunity do these defendants have?
## Holding
- Remanded to lower courts.
- State executive branch officials have different levels of **qualified immunity**, depending on the scope of discreiton and responsibility they have.
	- Local officers / cops: "good faith and probable cause"
	- Higher officials: uhhh it depends. More leeway when there's "civil disorder" bc that requires a prompt response and there's no consensus on how to act.
- Reasoning:
	- It is unjust to subject an officer to liability for actions which require discretion
	- the threat of liability would deter officers from exercising decisiveness and good judgement.
- Historically, we have found official immunity for legislators and judges..

# Absolute Immunity
- Absolute immunity = plaintiff cannot recover damages, no matter what.
- Judicial, Legislative conduct = absolute immunity. Executive/Administrative conduct = qualified immunity.
	- Not about specific job titles, but the offending conduct.
	- **But some administrative conduct may receive absolute immunity if it leans hard on the discretion thing**

### Judicial Immunity
> [!info] When a judge performs a judicial **function**, they get absolute immunity
> Judicial immunity is absolute - even if a judge blatantly violates the constitution, they receive absolute immunity. 
> BUT, this is about function, not the job descrption. A judge who is performing a non-judicial thing as part of their job only receives qualified immunity.

> [!info] Jurors and law clerks also receive judicial immunity
- ***Stump v. Sparkman*** - absolute immunity for a judge ordering a sterilization on a teenage girl for no reason.
	- The girl's mom went to the judge and asked them for an order to get the girl sterilized.
	- The daughter was not told she was going in for a sterilization.
- ***Forrester v. White*** - qualified immunity for a judge firing an employee for discriminatory reasons.
	- This is an *administrative function*

### Not Adjudicatory Enough
- ***Cleavinger v. Saxner*** - qualified immunity for corrections officers who were hearing disciplinary actions of inmates.
	- They were too closely affiliated with the people bringing the complaints - other corrections officers - and thus conducting an administrative action, and not an adjudicatory one.
	- IE, they were not adjudicative enough 
	- NOTE: this is about this specific prison board. Some prison and parole boards are found to be adjudicatory enough to receive absolute immunity.

### Immunity of Prosecutors
> [!info] Prosecutors can also get absolute immunity, depending on their function.
> Only for functions that are "intimately associated with a criminal trial" (*Imbler*)

- ***Imbler v. Pacthman*** - absolute immunity for acts "intimately associated with judicial phase of criminal process," qualified immunity for "investigative or administrative activities"

- ***Burns v. Reed*** - Prosecutor hypnotized plaintiff without her awareness and got a confession that way.
	- Qualified immunity for charge against prosecutor for approving request to use hypnosis, because it is an *investigation*
	- Absolute immunity for charge against investigator for misleading court finding - that he admitted improper evidence into court.
- ***Van de Kamp v. Goldstein*** - released prisoner sued LA County DA for failure to train subordinates (prosecutors) to disclose impeachment material to him. 
	- **Holding**: Absolute immunity!
	- **Supervising a trial is intimately associated with a criminal trial.**


## Why do some functions get absolute immunity while others get qualified immunity?
- Cynical reading: the people making the rules about judges and prosecutors are themselves judges, and they probably used to be prosecutors at some point.
- Less cynical reading:
	- In a lot of these errors, there is an alternative remedy!
	- If a prosecutor hurts you, they're being overseen by a judge.
	- If a judge hurts you in some way, you can appeal!
	- Plus, you're answerable to the bar! You can get sanctioned or disbarred!
