## Pullman Abstention Doctrine
- Pullman abstention comes up when a plaintiff is bringing both a federal constitutional claim, and a separate state law claim.
- If the state law claim is *ambiguous*, not obviously clear how federal courts should apply it, then we would prefer it if state courts decided on that issue!
- And the state law issue can resolve the case.
- Result: federal court stays the case until a state court figures this out!
- Certification: instead of invoking *Pullman*, the federal court can ask the state's supreme court for an advisory opinion on the state law issue. Modern alternative to Pullman abstention, much faster.
- There is also a federal statute that allows federal appellate courts to ask SCOTUS for an advisory opinion on an issue, but SCOTUS has limited the power of that statute so much that it's effectively non-existent nowadays.

# Younger v. Harris
- Younger is arrested for distributing leaflets, sues in federal court for free speech violation.

 - Anti-Injunction Act: a federal court may not grant an injunction to state proceedings at a state court unless:
	 - Congress explicitly authorizes it;
	 - Where necessary in need of its jurisdiction; or
	 - To protect or effectuate its judgments.
- SCOTUS does not follow the Anti-Injunction Act in *Younger*.
- Instead, they abstain from hearing *Younger* because
	- There's an adequate remedy here for the plaintiff : The state criminal courts can hear his constitutional claims and provide him with the remedy.
