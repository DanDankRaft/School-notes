# Review from Last Class
## Intro to Bivens
- For suing *federal officials* - always in their **personal capacity**
- Only remedy is damages!
	- And since its in their personal capacity - the money only comes out of their own bank account!
## The Test
1. Has S. Ct. recognized this type of *Bivens* claim before?
	- 4th amendment (*Bivens*)
	- 5th amendment - only equal protection (*Davis*)
	- 8th amendment (*Carlson*)
2. Is *Bivens* precluded?
	- Is there an alternative remedy?
		- Look to *Carlson* - an alternative statute with express exclusive remedy provision, that is as good as *Bivens* (this last point is de facto overturned at this point)??
		- But it's been weakened over time - *Bush v. Lucas*, *Schweiker*, *Castaneda*
	- Are there any special factors?
		- E.g. - the military (*Chappell*, *Stanley*)

- Main takeaway from today: Bivens is technically still around, but it's been weakened a ton, especially via special factors.

# Westfall Act (1988)
- Before *Bivens*, the way you sued federal officials violating your rights was via a tort lawsuit under state common law.
	- Then, the defendants would always remove it to federal court and argue that they're just doing their job
	- Then, the plaintiff would retort by saying that the defendant violated the constitution.
- *Bivens* allows you to sue directly in federal court for constitutional violations.
- But until 1988, the old way still existed as an option.

### The Act
- Federal Tort Claims Act is the "exclusive remedy" for tortious injuries caused by federal officials, acting within the scope of their office or employment.
- In effect:
	- By making it the exclusive remedy - barring state tort actions!
	- Main trigger: officials must be acting within the scope of their employment. Which is an easy bar to clear, esp when your boss backs you.
- BUT, exception left out for Constitutional claims!
	- *Bivens* is still around!

- Main goal: make lawsuits against federal officials simpler.
- At the time, *Bivens* was still pretty broad, so Congress didn't think they were cutting off remedies. But now...

> [!info] Today, the only way to sue a federal official and get damages is ***Bivens***
> And since *Bivens* is so narrow...

# Ziglar v. Abbasi
582 US ___ (2017)
- Plaintiffs were unlawfully detained ("hold until cleared" policy) in the aftermath of 9/11.
- Sued two categories - executive officials (AG, FBI Director, ICE Commissioner), and their prison wardens.
- Claims under 5th Amendment Due Process.
	- Why not 8th amendment? Because they weren't charged with anything, so this isn't a punishment.

- Throwback: this case seems really similar to *Iqbal* from civpro. This is because *Iqbal* was about 9/11!
## Legal Question
- Was this a "new context" not covered by prior *Bivens* claims?

## Holding
- Yes, and therefore there were no *Bivens* grounds here.

- You ask if this is a "new context" that SCOTUS hasn't seen before for a *Bivens* claim. And if it is, you run it through the *Bivens* analysis.

- What can make something a "new context"?
	- Implicate a different constitutional right from previous caselaw.
	- Is this a different factual pattern?
	- Level of official being sued - how high up are they?
	- Did judicial precedent fail to provide a clear guide for what to do here?
	- "Special factors," such as:
		- National security
		- Alternative remedies.

> [!important] Courts should just be very weary to apply Bivens. We don't like expanding Bivens.

- If it's not a new context, **no new analysis needs to be done**
- But if it is, we analyze this case in consideration of the "special factors"

- Analyzing the special factors present here:
	- National security context - post 9/11!
	- Alternative remedies:
		- Habeas corpus would've provided an alternative remedy
		- *Lack of Alternative Remedy Created by Congress* is an argument in favor of precluding *Bivens* here. There was high public awareness about this issue, so if Congress wanted to do something about this, they would've done it.
## Dissent
- *Bivens* is especially important in national security contexts, bc the nature of damages litigation means it will be heard in court after the emergency/emotional circumstances subsided...

# Hernandez v. Mesa
589 US ___ (2020)
- A group of Mexican teenagers played by running up to the border, touching the fence, then running back, plaintiff's son was shot and killed by a border patrol officer.
- The US conducted an internal investigation but decided not to file any charges against the officer.
- There was a dispute - Mexico wanted to extradite the border patrol officer involved, the US refused.
- Finally, the parents sued the officer under *Bivens*, for 4th and 5th amendment violations.
## Legal Question
- This this a "new context"?
### Holding
- Yes! And i
- Contributing factors:
	- Foreign national is injured! The plaintiff is from Mexico.
	- Special factors:
		- National security context - esp. with extradition dispute
		- Foreign relations context - see above.
		- Alternative remedies: ....

> [!important] Notice how there was no alternative remedy here. The parents only opted for a civil suit after they exhausted all other alternatives!

> [!info] Strong signals from the court that they want to overturn *Bivens*
> They don't want to award damages in a context where Congress hasn't created a remedy!

# Egbert v. Boule
596 US ___ (2022)
- Plaintiff was harassed by a border patrol agent. In response to filing a complaint, defendant launched a retaliatory IRS audit of Plaintiff's taxes???
- Plaintiff sued under *Bivens*, 4th and 1st amendment claims
### Legal Questions
- Is there a "new context" here?
## Holding
- Plaintiff loses

- Special factors:
	- National security
	- Alternative remedies:
		- Border patrol has an administrative process for disciplining officers.
		- Implicating different constitutional rights: right to complain under 1st amendment, and something else under 4th amendment.

- Justice Thomas: Bivens is about whether there is any reason to think that Congress might be better equipped to create a damages remedy
# Factors contributing to "new context" or avoidance of Bivens (NOTE I WROTE THIS BEFORE CLASS MIGHT NOT BE ACCURATE)

- National security context

	- If not, is it bc Congress probably doesn't wanna provide one?
	- If not, is Congress better equipped to create one?
