# Review from Last Class
- Just looking at the text of AEDPA and 1983, the statutes seem to overlap! BUT
- SO, how do these statutes coexist? There are some contexts where only federal habeas can exist. Any litigant would rather be in 1983 and not habeas.
- *Preiser*: When someone is challenging the fact or duration of their confinement, only federal Habeas can be used. Even though it's harder to bring habeas nowadays.
- *Heck*: When someone is asking for damages, you have to use 1983, but you can't do it in a way that implies the invalidity of your conviction or sentence, unless that conviction or sentence was otherwise overturned.
	- If a claim *may* invalidate a conviction or sentence (e.g., asking for DNA evidence that may further incriminate you), it's not barred!
	- If your claim is that a statute is invalid, and you bring this claim after you already served a sentence, that does not imply that the conviction was invalid!
- You can also bring 1983 claims for challenges to the specific conditions of your confinement (e.g., not being served kosher food) as long as it's not challenging the underlying sentence.

# *Strickland v. Washington* ineffective assistance of counsel test
1. Counsel's performance "fell below an objective standard of reasonableness"
	- Strategic decisions (decisions about micro-scale things that usually don't involve decisionmaking from the) are not covered here.
2. Counsel's "deficient performance prejudiced the defense" - reasonable performance that but for the deficient performance, the outcome would've been different.
	- Actually made a difference in the outcome, in a way that hurt the client.
