# Appealability of Immunity Denials
- Immunity is *immunity from suit* - you should not be subject to the lawsuit at all!

## *Mitchell v. Forsyth*
472 U.S. 511 (1985)
- Former federal A.G. was sued for illegal wiretapping. District court rejected qualified immunity for him.
- He tried appealing qualified immunity before the case was decided on the merits.
### Legal Q:
- Can you appeal a denial of official immunity before a decision on the merits?
### Holding
- Yes!
- **Appeals are immediately appealable**
	- This is different from the typical civpro situation, where you can only appeal a final judgment.
	- A *denial of immunity* is not a final judgment because you can still win on the merits.
- *Even qualified immunity* is about immunity from suit - not a defense from liability.

## *Johnson v. Jones*
515 US 304 (1995)
- Plaintiff sued cops for beating him up, using §1983
- Defendants moved for SJ on lack of evidence and were denied.
- Defendants then immediately appealed on grounds of qualified immunity.
- **Holding:** appeal of qualified immunity decisions only makes sense if "a given set of facts violates clearly established law," NOT if there is still a genuine question of fact.
	- "A simple 'we didn't do it' case" is the latter.
- **IE, the appeal has to assume that the defendant wins even if everything the plaintiff alleges is true**

- Nowadays, official immunity is brought up in a 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss.
- You can argue immunity at different stages in litigation. You can raise it over and over again as the plaintiff amends their complaint in light of the evidence!
- Sovereign immunity is also immediately appealable.

# Legislative and Presidential Immunity
- For the most part, federal official immunity under *Bivens* is the same as state and local immunity under §1983, and the caselaw on it is considered interchangeable, with some exceptions:
	- Members of Congress - criminal immunity (with some exceptions)
	- The President (see below)

# Speech and Debate Clause: Article 1, § 6, cl. 1
> The Senators and Representatives .... shall in all Cases, except Treason, Felony and Breach of the Peace, be privileged from Arrest during their Attendance at the Session of their respective Houses.
- Grants legislators absolute immunity for all *legislative functions* - including congressional speech and debate, but also research, congressional investigations, hearings, etc.
- Very broad in application!
- Important case *Gravel v. United States* - about Mike Gravel publishing the Pentagon Papers by reading them into the legislative record

# Da President
- The president often gets absolute immunity
- despite how no clause in the constitution give the president any immunity.
## *Nixon v. Fitzgerald*
457 US 731 (1982)
- Fitzgerald alleges that he was fired from his government job because of his disloyalty to the administration.
- **Holding**: Presidents are absolutely immune from damages for all acts within the **outer perimeter** of his duties. **Governors don't get that!**
	- This is a *prudential* decision - the president is an easy, high profile target for lawsuits, and if we let people sue him all the time it will get in the way of him getting stuff done.
	- This feels like it's more about the office, not the function!
> [!info] President gets absolute immunity for all acts within the "outer perimeter" of his office

## *Clinton v. Jones*
520 US 681 (1997)
- Plaintiff sued Pres. Clinton during his presidency, for stuff he did before he was president. 
- **Holding:** That's allowed.
	- The "sound discretion" of district judges will protect Pres. from harassment, frivolous litigation.
> [!info] Only qualified immunity for acts taken prior to his presidency
> We trust the sound judgment of district court judges to determine whether a lawsuit against a sitting president makes sense.

> [!info] Governors only get qualified immunity for their conduct

## *Trump v. Vance*
__ U.S. __ (2020)
- NY District Attorney subpoenaed Trump's accounting firm during a criminal investigation.
	- Not on the president itself!
- Trump intervenes and says he has absolute immunity from a state criminal process. He wants to enjoin the state court from asking for this information.
- *Younger* abstention - does it kick in here?
	- District court found that *Younger* exception applies.
	- Second circuit affirmed in part, but found that *Younger* exception doesn't apply when the the United States or the President seeks an injunction against a state criminal proceeding.
	- SCOTUS didn't say anything about *Younger* here..
### Holding
- Prosecutor could get the subpoena, no immunity from state criminal prosecution.
- Rationale: same as *Clinton v. Jones*. This is not a big deal.

## Legal Q
- Is the president immune from federal criminal prosecution?
### Holding
- Yes for some claims, remanded for others.
- Presidents have absolute immunity for exercises of their "core constitutional powers":
	- Pardons,
	- Removing executive officers
	- Recognition of foreign governments
	- Other things mentioned in the constitution
- "Presumptive" immunity for acts "within the outer perimeter of his official responsibility" (IE, other "official acts.")
	- This presumption can be refuted, even for an official act, if the prosecution can show that criminal prosecution here would pose "no danger of intrusion on the authority and functions of the Executive Branch."
- **No immunity** for "unofficial acts"
- Unofficial v. official:
	- Do NOT look into the President's motives
	- Merely violating a generally applicable law is not sufficient to make an act unofficial.
- Application here:
	- Any conduct that's about discussions with Justice Department officials - an official act.
	- Any interaction with persons outside the executive branch - REMANDED.

- Also, there is a distinction between the president acting in his capacity as a president, and his capacity as a candidate for office.

- Finally, cannot even introduce any evidence concerning conduct for which Pres. is immune, even for the purpose of supporting other charges.


# What is the "outer perimeter"?
- Very broad and not clearly defined.
- Things that are not explicitly mentioned in the constitution but are generally understood to be part of the president's job.
- Even when taking a vacation in camp david, they might still be holding important talks or giving orders.
- If the president shoots someone in broad daylight, in the middle of 5th avenue, does that fall within the "outer perimeter" of his official duties? Maybe!
	- He is the commander in chief of the military, directing the use of violence is part of his job.
	- Maybe he did it to protect his own life? Protecting the president's life is one of the duties of (some members) of the executive branch, and the president is the head of the executive branch!
