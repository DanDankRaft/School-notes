Chief Justice of Texas Supreme Court: Jimmy Blacklock 
SCOTX Justice since 2018, Chief Justice since 2025

# The Courts
- In the UK, the judicial power was derivative of the executive and legislative powers.
- "It is emphatically the power of the courts to say what the law is" - Marbury v. Madison. A common saying 
	- Blacklock - the job of the court is to *issue judgments* and *decide cases*. To the extent that they say what the law is, it is only insofar as it is necessary to issue judgments. Issuing opinions is not really "the court's power"
	- Just because the court says that the law is X does not mean that the law is actually X. 
		- Lower courts follow these kinds of precedents bc following the lead of the higher courts is necessary to ensure that the legal system is uniform and consistent. Not because the precedent *is the law*.
- He supports Departmentalism.
	- Lingering question: what does that mean if the court has one opinion about what interpretation of the law is correct, but the executive or legislative branch disagree? Are the executive or legislative branches going against the law by ignoring the court? Should the court's opinions be *enforced*?
- On Stare Decisis - "I'm more of a Thomas than a Scalia." Precedent is important for *prudential* reasons, but precedent is not binding in the sense that the court's past interpretation of the constitution is "the law" in any meaningful way.
	- Especially on constitutional questions! Upholding a wrongful interpretation of the constitution is taking away power from the people!
- If the court decides something wrong, can't the people just amend it? Why is it such a big deal to uphold the democratic will of the people? Bc it's still placing a huge burden on them!

- How does departmentalism work in a plural executive? Nobody knows!

- The court thinks its not its job to stick its nose in fights between the legislative and executive branches

- "The judicial power of this State shall be vested in one Supreme Court, . . . and in such other courts as may be provided by law. The legislature may establish such other courts as it may deem necessary and prescribe the jurisdiction and organization thereof, and may conform the jurisdiction of the district and other inferior courts thereto."
	- Putting the thumb on the scale in favor of the legislature - common in legislatures of the time!
