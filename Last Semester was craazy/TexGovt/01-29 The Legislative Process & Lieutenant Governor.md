# Basics of the Legislative Process
- Texas is *very unusual* for only holding regular sessions every other year
- First 30 days - only bill filing
- Next 30 days - only committee considerations
- Legislative Counsels are the legal counsels of each member of the legislature. They have AC privilege.
- For a bill to pass on third reading in the Senate, you have to put a notice of intent on the calendar over it for three days.''

# Local Bills
- Constitution only allows local bills for a few specific purposes. Requires notice in affected area before being introduced.
- Local bills = only apply to a specific region
### Bracket Bills
- Bracket bill = de facto local bill that defines the region by some classification device or criterion (ie, only applying to counties with a population greater than 3 million)
- Bracket bills can theoretically have any purpose, but SCOTX devised a test for whether bracket bills are constitutional.
- Bracket bills can still fail on a point of order if they don't have a published notice!!

- Bracket bill test:
	- Must uniformly apply
	- something
	- something

# Interim Studies
- At the end of each regular session, each chamber's presiding officer (speaker of the house, lt. governor) assigns each standing committee a list of items to study in the interim.
- This means they have 19 months!
- They have time and budget to hold hearings, take testimony, consult with experts, and perform other research. The committees often travel across the state and across the country for their research.
- Committee ultimately issues a report over their research topics.
	- Can include proposed legislation
- Nowadays, the first question legislators ask before filing a bill is whether there was an interim study on the subject & what it said.

# Lieutenant Governor
- Constitutional powers:
	- President of the Senate
	- May debate and vote when Senate reolves into committee of the whole
	- break a tie vote
	- acting governor when governor leaves state, becomes incapacitated, or when the office of governor becomes vacant.
- Most of his de facto power comes from Senate rules
	- Why? Bc we don't have a majority leader and minority leader in the Senate. If we did, lt. governor would have most of their power stripped from them and put into a majority leader (and to lesser extent, minority leader)
	- Why do we not have a majority and minority?
		- Bipartisanship
		- Continuity and precedent
		- What happens if you initiate a power stripping attempt and fail? Lt. governor will have a vendetta against you
- Powers granted by Senate rules to the *President of the Senate*:
	- Decide all questions of order (subject to appeal)
	- Have control over all physical parts of the Capitol set aside for the Senate
	- Right to name members to perform certain duties
- *Recognition*: exclusive power to recognize members and set daily agenda. Takes on the role of the calendars committee. Gives him much more direct power to kill legislation than the speaker of the house.
- *Bill Referral and Committee Appointments*: full power over both of those!
	- Including which standing and special committees exist.
	- Including what each committee's jurisdiction is. Most frequently, Senate rules don't outline the jurisdiction of a committee, so the lt. governor can send each bill wherever he wants.
	- Including appointments to all conference committees.
	- Has zero restrictions on which members he can appoint to which committee, except for the total number of members in each committee.

## Suspending Regular Order of Business
- Bc of the blocking bill, you need to suspend the rules in order to do anything
- Before 2015, you needed 2/3 to suspend regular order of business.
- In 2015, Dan Patrick convinced Senate to reduce threshold to 3/5
- In 2019, after Dems made some gains, Dan Patrick reduced threshold again to 5/9.

- Typically they don't apply the suspension rules to special sessions.
