- Position established in Texas Constitution, Article 4, Section 1
- Statewide elected position - as opposed to a unitary elected executive position
- Article 4, section 22: AG shall represent the state in all suits in SCOTUS in which teh state may be a party.
- Govt code: AG shall represent the state in SCOTUS and courts of appeal
- Article 4, Section 22: AG shall "perform such other duties as may be required by law" - interpreted to include representation in district courts.

- AG is the state's lawyer in litigation!
- But unlike a regular attorney-client relationship, the AG has complete control over his relationship with the state. He can even pursue a legal position antithetical to the interests of the state agency.
	- This is an intentional byproduct of the divided executive branch!
	- Example: Ken Paxton represented a local clerk against overseeing state agency for refusing to officiate same sex marriages.

# The Attorney General-Litigation Authority

- SCOTUS recognizes this as well - *Hollingsworth v. Perry*. If petitioners are representing on behalf of a state, but that state's AG refuses to appeal, then the appeal lacks standing!


- AG has exclusive right to represent state agencies, they can only hire other lawyers "in subordination" to his authority.
- This includes both agency attorneys and outside counsel.
- Exception: a constitutionally mandated agency. 

- Limits to AG authority in extreme circumstances:
	- *Hill v. Texas Water Quality Board*; AG filed suit against state agency seeking to set aside agency orders. Court of Appeals: can't sue a state agency.
	- WHY: AG can sue a state agency and then refuse to let them bring outside counsel.
	- Exception: *Open Records Act*. AG litigates against a state agency if the agency disputes an Open Records decision issued by the AG.

## Supersedeas Rule
- Supersedeas rule: AG can appeal a judge's order granting a temporary injunction against the State and "supersede" the court's order, preventing the court from enforcing the injunction for the duration of the case.
	- 2023 example: appealed three different injunctions issued by district judges on the state's abortion ban directly to SCOTX.
	- 2026 example: the Dallas county voting deadline extension thing during the senate primary

## Entering Settlements
- *Terrazas v. Ramirez* - includes authority to propose and negotiate a settlement agreement in action attacking the constitutionality of a reapportionment statute.
- HOWEVER< this pwoer does not permit the AG to draw and effectuate a reapportionment of state legislative districts. Only the trial court judge can do that, and only if the legislature fails. Why? Separation of powers!

# Contested Cases in Stage Agencies
- Tons of state agencies have administrative tribunals!
- But a lot of them use judges from State office of Administrative Hearings
- Not all administrative hearings in Texas are conducted by SOAH. Rather, a number of agencies continue to have their in-house personnel to conduct hearings.
- In-house Administrative Law Judges (ALJs) are subject-matter experts bc they only hear cases in their agencies.
- In contrast, SOAH hears cases from 50+ agencies, but they're more impartial in adjudication against the agency!
