# Why does a VC firm run war games?
- Most bigger VCs eventually get policy shops that do things like write white papers.
	- Traditional thinking: They treat policy as a problem of information transfer. If only we get the right facts in front of the right people, good policy will follow.
	- Traditional thinking is wrong.
- There is no such thing as unscripted policy.

- There is a disconnect between the people who are knowledgeable about AI and the people who are knowledgeable about public policy?
	- It's also about developing *strategic empathy* - understanding each others' roles!

## About Lux Capital
- Small firm: ~$7b in assets, 40 employees.
- Invests in a lot of different stages:
	- Investing in early startups that they expect to grow big!
	- Pre-seed, seed, A, B, C, D, etc.
- Only invests in: physical sciences, biomedical sciences, computational sciences (AI compute products)
	- Osmo - startup that digitizes scent. Including letting machines identify scents that humans cannot smell! Ex: it enables a computer to smell fentanyl. 
	- Startup that uses pigs to make human kidney transplants.


- Policy point: think of the policy implications if their weird startups become successful.
	- Ex: organ transplants are the biggest moneymakers for hospitals. If pig transplants become cheap and available, what do we do?

- They evaluate hundreds of companies a year and pick a few to invest in. They try to do pattern recognition, looking 2-8 years ahead to see what's going to be big at that point. Faster timeframe than regulation.
	- Even if government was really good at talking to founders the way VCs are, they wouldn't be fast enough bc that's not where the market pressure is placed on them.
	- He thinks that this is the origin of regulatory lag.

- There's a deeper epistemological problem: the people writing the rules are regulating with outdated mental models. 
	- The solution: putting people at the edge of technological development (incl. startup founders) at the same room as policymakers. Going closer than conferences and white papers, you need personal contact!
	- Problem: if you put policymakers and founders in the same place, don't you risk convincing the policymakers that this technology shall not come to pass? Answer: usually the opposite happens. Fear mostly comes from lack of communication and lack of knowledge.

# Riskgaming

- Game theory presumes that all actors act in their rational self interest. But that doesn't really happen in the real world. People are irrational all the time.
	- "Game theory is not good enough to be useful, but it's just good enough to be trouble."
- Riskgaming is all about forcing people to make decisions with incomplete information and a ton of pressure, and then forcing them to live with the consequences of their actions!


- Dopamine is about prediction error - it's not triggered when you get a reward, but when expect to receive a reward. It goes down when you don't get a reward that you expected.
- Dopamine is an error signal. The signal is stronger when the uncertainty is highest.

- Two types of games:
	- Full Immersion - 6-10 people playing for 3-4 hours. It's an executive retreat.
	- Salon Experience - 10-50 people playing for 1-2 hours. It's a cocktail networking event.
