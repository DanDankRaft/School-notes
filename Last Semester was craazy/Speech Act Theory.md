- Illocutionary act - a complete speech act - the kinds of acts that are not protected by the first amendment (638)
- Perlocutionary act - a speech act, as defined by the effect it has on the listener (638)

- Propositional act - utterance act + propositional meaning.
- Utterance Act - the physical action that creates the speech - producing sound as part of speaking, typing text on a computer, marching in a parade (639)
- **propositional meaning** - the conceptual meaning of an utterance act. For example, typing an algorithm is an utterance act, whose meaning is that algorithm. (639)

- On intent - an illocutionary act includes two types of intent:
	- The intent to express some message to an audience - not sufficient to make an act illocutionary.
	- The intent to perform the act that's being performed.
- Ex: "I promise to mow the lawn":
	- Intent to express - intent to tell someone "I promise to mow the lawn" and have them understand me.
	- Intent to perform - intent to make a promise that I will mow the lawn.

- Sincerity Condition - when you make a speech act, you are always expressing something about your internal mental state. The sincerity condition is that this expression about your internal state is true. Basically, that you're not lying. (641)
	- Ex: when you promise to mow the lawn, the sincerity condition is that you actually intend to mow the lawn.
- Meaning Intention - speech acts are unique because they always involve some representation of reality. The meaning intention is what you intend to represent. (641)
	- Ex: when you say "I promise to mow the lawn," the meaning intention is to express that you intend to mow the lawn.

> [!info] Unless you're not lying, the sincerity condition and meaning intention are the same

- Meaning Intention has two aspects: (641)
	- Intention to represent - what you're trying to represent about the state of the owrld
	- Intention to communicate - what you're trying to communicate.
- If you believe in subtlety, those intents might not be the same:
	- Ex: "it's raining"
	- Intent to represent - represent that it's raining
	- Intent to communicate - tell my wife that i'm not going to walk the dog


- The point of community is that it establishes conventions which help us determine what the meaning of speech is (642-46)
	- For example, a language (like English) is a social convention that helps us derive meaning out of certain sounds and visuals.
