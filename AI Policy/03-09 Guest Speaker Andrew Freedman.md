## Lessons from being Cannabis Czar
- **Don't overburden the regulatory system!!**
	- In Cannabis - everyone wanted both really strict standards for licensing but also wanted all the businesses to be successful.
- **You don't have an idea in regulatory law unless that idea is implementable**
	- The system is often not handled to handle the problems that we're trying to solve with them.

- Rn, the political discourse is so contentious that it gets in the way of rulemaking. And policymakers are aware of that!
	- In the past, technologies have had a honeymoon period where different parties who disagreed with each other on the big picture still agreed to work together to solve commonly-acknowledged emergent problems.
	- This no longer exists at large, and especially with AI.
	- Ex: Parties that are completely anti-regulation refuse to work with anyone else, labeling them as "doomers"
- Populist AI skepticism and doomers are not the same!

- There's a way to use AI populism to give yourself leverage in policy debates.. but it's also very dangerous and hard to control.

- Anti-AI populism is a very recent force! Up until about 18 months ago, this force was completely non-existent.
- Up until now, pushback to AI optimism (in policy circles) completely came from the safety/doomerist camp. 


# IVOs
- Independent Verification Organizations - government would license third-party, independent verifiers, who could demonstrate that a specific product or technology produces less harm than the status quo or the most likely alternative.
	- Ex: can demonstrate if an AI chatbot produces less suicidal ideation among teens than a human counselor.
- Advantage for stakeholders: IVOs provide evidence for *standard of care*, extremely strong for winning negligence lawsuits.

- Advantage as a policy: it's entirely outcomes-based. It's really easy to explain to policymakers what this does and how it's helpful without them having to know anything about 

- IVOs vs existing product certifications (UL, ETL):
	- They're actually very similar!

- Regulatory capture is a potential harm in any government oversight!
	- He thinks: the best way to do this is ensure that an IVO which certifies products that have bad outcomes, that IVO should lose their license.


- Downside of an outcome-driven policy: if you're a company, that's way harder to comply with!
- Downside of private sector IVOs - What if your IVO goes under? What if your IVO turns out to have been fudging their numbers?

- Steamboat inspection problem: the inspector is paid by the people they're inspecting...
