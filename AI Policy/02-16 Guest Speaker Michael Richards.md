## Private Rights of Action
- This bill has no private right of action. This bill is quite explicit about saying that there's no private right of action. Richards likes that! Other laws make it unclear that there might be a backdoor private right of action
- AG even has rulemaking authority. The combination of exclusive AG enforcement + full AG rulemaking authority is bad for corporations. The plaintiff who sues you gets to decide what the rules are.

- Richards' thinking about private rights of action: it's a boondoggle for the bar.
	- Private right of action is usually used against small and medium sized businesses, whereas AG enforcement is used against big businesses.
	- AG uses a lot of discretion before commencing a suit, figuring out if they have a good chance of winning. Versus private right of action, in which the plaintiff's interest is in commencing suit whenever there's nonzero chance of winning.

# Enforcement
- At the same time that the law passed, an AI working group was statutorily created. It ended up becoming the de facto working group to amend this bill in the future.
- It became clear that there was not agreement between members of the working group and other stakeholders on most contentious provisions of SB 205
- So as a result, Sen. Rodriguez (author of original SB-205) decided he would introduce a new bill to amend SB-205. He ended up introducing a new bill at the tail end of the 2025 session. There was urgency to pass it by the end of the session bc otherwise it would come into effect before 2026. But then Sen. Rodriguez killed his own bill bc it didn't work out.

- The law is not really enforced yet bc the AG is running for governor and doesn't want to set the rules that he's supposed to set. 
- Currently, law is supposed to come into effect on June 30th

- In 2025, there was a special session. The legislature quickly polarized into different camps around this issue. There were five different bills filed to amend SB-205.

- There's currently a working group with 15 different groups with voting power, under a mediator on finding an agreement on how to amend SB-205.

- This dynamic of *trying to be the first* leads to bad policy bc you're constraining yourself due to an artificially tight timeline.

# Impact on other states
- Other states are reading SB 205 as a disaster. A lot of different states that proposed copies of it are now pulling back.

- Since AG is an elected position, there are a lot of political considerations in which rules he chooses to adopt.


- Ricahards' thinking here: the bill establishes a cause of action for a certain harm, his questions are:
	- What is that harm
	- How can we actually address that harm
	- How do existing regulations fall short in addressing that harm as it comes from AI?


# Congress v. State Legislatures
- It is way easier to get a bill passed via a state legislature than Congress.
- The biggest enemy to passing legislation in Congress is the other chamber.
- There is a ton of overlap between different committees in Congress, so you end up having tons of overlapping hearings over the same bill.


# On small business exceptions
- Argument for small business exceptions: There's very little empirical evidence that small business exceptions actually change the behavior of a small business.
- Very small startups tend to not get tort insurance. 


- Informational cybersecurity reviews, etc are often very performative because they're mandated by insurance, which is motivated by the law.
