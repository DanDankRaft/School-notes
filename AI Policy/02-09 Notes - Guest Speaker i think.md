# What are we worried about?
- Systemic Risks - an EU term already used in financial regulation.
	- "Any risk that is **specific to** the **high-impact capabilities** of general-purpose AI models, having a significant impact on the unin market due to their reach, or due to actual or reasonable foreseeable negative effects on public health, safety, public security, fundamental rights, or the society as a whole, that can be propagated at scale across the value chain."
- High-impact capabilities - capabilities that match or exceed the capabilities recorded in the **most advanced general-purpose AI models**.
	- It's difficult to evaluate and define which model is the most advanced given any specific capabilities.
	- Ex: it's difficult to use benchmarks bc they're unreliable, can be easily gamed, difficult to evaluate what types of capabilities a benchmark measures.
	- Optional presumption: "A general-purpose AI model shall be **presumed** to have high impact capabilities ... when the cumulative amount of computation used for its training measured in floating point operations is greater than **10^25**"
		- Compute is a good proxy bc only large, advanced companies have access to this level of compute.
		- EU AI office was set up right before the AI act was passed. At the time, the AI Safety Agency was very small and staffed by people with very little knowledge.

- Recital 110 - "General-purpose AI models could pose systemic risks which include, but are not limited to, any actual or reasonably foreseeable negative effects in relation to major accidents, disruptions of critical sectors.... a billion other things"
> [!important] RECITALS ARE NOT LEGALLY BINDING. THEY MERELY GUIDE INTERPRETATION!!
> Recitals are a common way to give lobbyists, special interests, think tanks, etc, something that looks like a win to them, but that doesn't have any legal force.

# Code of Practice - What do we do with systemic risk?
- "providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk shall **assess and mitigate possible systemic risks.**"
- 13 chairs (independent experts) were appointed to draft a binding *code of conduct* for AI models that carry high systemic risk
- Article 56: "If, by 2 August 2025, a code of practice cannot be finalized, or **if the AI office deems that it is not adequate** ... the Commission may provide, by means of implementing acts, **common rules** for the implementation of the obligations."
	- Nobody knows what 'common rules' means. This is not some technical EU term.
- The code of practice had many signatories. Notable exceptions: Meta, xAI (only signed safety and security chapter)

- Signatories were "voluntold" to sign it. IE, there was an implication that if industry leaders wouldn't sign it, there would be negative consequences.

- Christoph - there's this perception within the EU Parliament that think tanks are representative of the public. He's doubtful of that.

# Does EU AI Act Preempt National Legislation
- Only used authority of Article 114 of the Treaty of the Functioning of the EU - which is about harmonizing rules across the European Union.
	- It's an open question as to whether member states can create national laws contradicting the EU AI act.
